Curatorial practice

History is a
living material.

Brian’s museum work connects collections, interpretation, and public learning. His curatorial interests center community memory and the histories held in everyday objects, textiles, and handmade forms.

01

Community Programming

Designed and led an activity for a Jackson community-care program using tea as a painting medium, creating a calm and accessible way for participants to make art together.

02

Archives & Collections

Cataloged and cared for artifacts in the Smith Robertson Museum and Cultural Center archives, working directly with objects that preserve Jackson’s community stories.

03

Digitization & Metadata

Created detailed collection records to preserve information about museum objects and make that knowledge more useful for future research, interpretation, and access.

04

Museum Education

Works as a gallery assistant and educational programmer at the Mississippi Civil Rights Museum and the Museum of Mississippi History, developing lesson plans and visitor worksheets.

❦

Material inquiry

Textile traditions connect Brian’s studio and museum practices. Draped figures, works on cloth, and the paper-garment performance treat clothing and fabric as records of labor, identity, memory, and transformation.

Meet the artist

Rooted in
Jackson.

Brian Hunter is an artist, curator, and museum professional based in Jackson, Mississippi. His work moves between painting, drawing, sculpture, performance, collections, and education.

His studio practice pairs bold color and theatrical materials with an interest in history and textile traditions. In museums, he extends that inquiry through archival care, interpretation, and programs that help communities engage with art and cultural memory.
